The red-boxed vehicle is stationary.

Decide the parking relationship of THIS red-boxed vehicle only.

IMPORTANT:
Do NOT choose A merely because parking lines or marked bays are visible somewhere near the vehicle.
The painted lines must geometrically belong to THIS vehicle's own parking position.

Use this decision order:

1. First check B:
Choose B if a painted parking-bay separator runs through or clearly underneath the vehicle's parking footprint, so substantial parts of the vehicle occupy TWO OR MORE marked bays.
A tiny tire touch or very small line crossing is NOT B.

2. Then check C:
Choose C if the vehicle itself is NOT geometrically inside one marked parking bay.
Examples include:
- open pavement,
- driving aisle,
- roadside,
- curbside,
- shoulder,
- beside marked bays rather than inside one,
- an unmarked gap beside parking bays,
- a curb or road edge with no marked bay around the vehicle.

A curb, road edge, or a single roadside line does NOT by itself establish a marked parking bay.

If marked bays are visible NEXT TO the vehicle but the vehicle is not between the boundaries of one of them, choose C.

3. Choose A only with positive evidence:
Choose A only when visible painted parking markings clearly establish ONE specific parking bay for THIS vehicle, and the vehicle is geometrically located within that same bay.

Minor line touching, a small tire crossing, slight bumper overhang, or mild angle can still be A if the vehicle clearly belongs to that one bay.

4. Choose D only if the image evidence is genuinely insufficient:
Use D when blur, darkness, occlusion, cropping, or severely missing markings make the relationship impossible to determine reliably.

Answer exactly one letter:

A = inside one marked bay
B = spans multiple marked bays
C = not in any marked bay
D = cannot tell
